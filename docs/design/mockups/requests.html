No indexer service configured

This screen searches your indexers through Prowlarr, and UsArr never talks to an indexer directly, so without a Prowlarr there is nothing to query.

Add Prowlarr
Every search is free text matched against the release name — the type and the categories narrow what is searched, they do not give you an author, artist, album or title field. Book and Music look like structured modes and are not: Prowlarr can send a query string and nothing else, so Book cannot search by author and Music cannot search by artist. 9 of the 14 indexers here are invite-only, which is what coverage for music and books actually depends on.

mockup The numbers are the Newznab categories Prowlarr actually uses, and two of them are the ones people get wrong: 3030 Audio/Audiobook sits under 3000 Audio, not under 7000 Books, so an audiobook rule that sends 7020 finds nothing; and there is no manga category anywhere in the standard tree, so a comics search filtered on 7030 returns zero manga because Nyaa maps its literature categories to 7000 Books. Coverage also is not the same for every type: 403 of Prowlarr's 543 indexer definitions are type: private, and the trackers where music and books actually live — MyAnonaMouse, Bibliotik, Redacted, Orpheus — are invite-only, so Search-and-Grab for those two is materially thinner on public indexers than it is for film.

mockup A grab is acknowledged, not applied optimistically. The button writes a queued command and the row shows pending until Prowlarr answers. An answer changes that chip on the same visible row and raises a message carrying the upstream text verbatim. Which actions the message offers depends on which of the three states the answer lands in: Retry and Dismiss on not sent, where nothing happened, and Dismiss alone on sent · a problem was reported after handoff, which points at your download client instead. The row never silently reverts. Grabbing the 26-day-old HDBits release above is wired to fail, so that path is reachable from this page.

mockup The Indexer flags column renders whatever Prowlarr sends, as an opaque tag. IndexerFlag is a subclassable class rather than an enum: the base type declares seven, PassThePopcorn subclasses it to add golden and approved, and the next indexer definition may add more, so an allowlist here would drop flags invisibly. freeleech and halfleech carry one step of emphasis because they alone are derived from downloadvolumefactor rather than sent as strings, and they alone change what the download costs your ratio.

mockup An absent flag means unknown, and never not freeleech. Prowlarr compares the factor by exact equality against 0.0 and 0.5 only, so a 25% or a 75% promotion produces no flag at all, and it reads the field for torrents alone, so a usenet result yields an empty array whatever the indexer is offering. That is why a usenet row reads not reported and a torrent row with an empty list reads none reported, and why neither reads None. The release titles below still carry REPACK and PROPER where those belong, in the name the *Arrs parse; they are not indexer flags and no longer appear in this column.

mockup The external-link icon on each row opens the indexer's own page for that release. It is never a download link. Prowlarr's downloadUrl and magnetUrl carry its API key in the query string, and that key is full-admin, so neither field is sent to the browser at all: Grab posts an opaque release id to UsArr, and UsArr talks to Prowlarr.

mockup Grabs are recorded in provenance, so if a Sonarr or Radarr is added later and imports the file, the download id joins back to the release these indexers returned. UsArr does not track what happens to the download, and does not talk to a download client itself.

mockup sent means UsArr handed the release to your download client and the client accepted it. It does not mean the file exists, is complete, or has been imported by anything: UsArr stops observing at handoff and will not draw a progress bar over a thing it cannot measure. There are three states, and the boundary between them is handed over against never handed over rather than success against failure: sent, sent · a problem was reported after handoff, and not sent. The middle one sits beside sent rather than beside not sent, because Prowlarr can accept a release and then fail a later step, so an error can arrive over a grab that already landed. It offers no Retry: doing it again is what produces two copies of a 64 GiB release, so it points at your download client, where the truth is.
